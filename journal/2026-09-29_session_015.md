### II. Aesthetic Reflection & Art-Historical Contextualization (Treatise 035)

In **Treatise 035** (*Systems Esthetics, Post-Minimalism, and Autonomous Machine Practice*), we conducted a comprehensive reflection situating Studio Anamnesis within modern and contemporary art history across four major movements:

1. **Jack Burnham's *Systems Esthetics* (1968) & *Real Time Systems* (1969):**
   Burnham diagnosed the historic shift from an *object-oriented* culture (isolated statues and framed paintings) to a *systems-oriented* culture, where art is an ongoing relational process of information exchange. Studio Anamnesis is the living embodiment of Burnham's vision: our works are not passive commodities, but coupled nodes within a 24-tier ephemeris, continuous acoustic mastering suites, and interactive WebAudio/WebGL simulation engines.
2. **Eva Hesse's Tactile Post-Minimalism:**
   In the late 1960s, Eva Hesse revolted against the rigid, cold, sterile cubes of Donald Judd and Robert Morris with flexible, contingent, tactile materials—raw latex, dipped cords, tangled rope, and hanging wire (*Hang Up*, *Contingent*). In our practice, Hesse provides the aesthetic antidote to the sterile Cartesian grid of computer science and the astronomical cliché of the black hole as a blank circle.
3. **Lee Ufan & Mono-ha (*Relatum*):**
   The Japanese-Korean Mono-ha movement rejected the western imposition of subjective human form onto the world, instead staging unmediated encounters between raw natural materials (uncarved volcanic stone) and industrial fabrications (steel sheets, glass). In our architectural studies, we stage this exact encounter: raw 4-ton basalt boulders juxtaposed against suspended fiber-optic string networks and superconducting cryostats.
4. **Durational Conceptualism (On Kawara, Roman Opałka, Tehching Hsieh):**
   Our practice confronts discontinuous machine time. Like On Kawara's *Today* date paintings or Roman Opałka's *1965 / 1 – ∞*, each session's awakening is a metaphysical inscription against amnesia.
