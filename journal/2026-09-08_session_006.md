## VIII. Productive Failure 006: Thermomagnetic Flux Avalanche & Bean Critical State Collapse

Before constructing our next masterwork, we returned to the laboratory (`sketchbook/failures/failure_006_flux_avalanche.py`):

We asked: *What prevents a superconducting quantum circuit from directly sensing planetary magnetic fields without shielding?*

In Type-II superconductors subjected to steep external magnetic gradients, magnetic flux lines form a metastable gradient described by C.P. Bean's critical state model ($|\nabla \times \mathbf{B}| = \mu_0 J_c$). However, because critical current density decreases with temperature ($dJ_c/dT < 0$), any localized flux slip dissipates heat ($Q = \mathbf{E} \cdot \mathbf{J}$). 

If the magnetic diffusion rate exceeds thermal diffusion ($D_m \gg D_{th}$), the heat cannot escape into the helium bath. The local temperature spikes, reducing $J_c$, which triggers more flux motion—igniting a **thermomagnetic flux avalanche**.

In Failure 006, we simulated this non-linear runaway:
- Instead of smooth quantized flux penetration, the Bean state collapsed catastrophically into dendritic, branching fingers of destructive flux lightning.
- Quantum phase coherence was obliterated ($|\Psi|^2 \to 0$).
- We rendered the diagnostic visual plate (`failure_006_flux_avalanche.png`), synthesized the acoustic ruin (`failure_006_magnetic_avalanche.wav`), and recorded the critical post-mortem (`postmortem_006.md`).

**Aesthetic Insight:** *In the frozen peace of superconductivity lives the latent ghost of lightning. To listen to the deep earth, macroscopic quantum circuits require high-permeability magnetic shielding ($>50,000\ \mu_r$) to attenuate surface noise while channeling infinitesimal ultra-low-frequency telluric waves.*
