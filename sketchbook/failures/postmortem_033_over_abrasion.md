## 1. The Phenomenon & Experimental Setup

In our laboratory exploration for INQ-34 (*The Porous Stele & The Generative Lacuna*), we attempted to replace naive pixel dropout (Draft A) with a physics-based model of aerodynamic grain saltation and mechanical shear stress.

To identify the phase boundary of viable weathering, we subjected a synthetic stone stele bearing inscribed glyphs to an extreme parameter sweep across kinetic energy and fluid shear stress:
$$\tau_{\text{shear}} = \frac{1}{2} C_d \rho v_{\text{wind}}^2$$
Where $v_{\text{wind}}$ was driven from moderate sub-critical velocities into super-critical overdrive ($v_{\text{wind}} > 4.5 \times v_c$), far exceeding the substrate's intrinsic cohesive yield stress:
$$\tau_{\text{shear}} \gg \sigma_{\text{yield}}(x, y)$$

## 3. The Law of Critical Weathering

From this failure, we derive the fundamental aesthetic theorem for INQ-34:

> **The Law of Critical Weathering:**  
> A stele cannot achieve poetic resonance through brute mechanical destruction. The generative lacuna exists **only in the sub-critical boundary regime** where the shear stress of the elements is tightly coupled to the differential hardness of the substrate:
> $$\langle \tau_{\text{shear}} \rangle \approx 0.85 \times \langle \sigma_{\text{yield}} \rangle$$
> At this critical balance:
> 1. Resilient quartz veins protect trailing lee zones, preserving islands of pristine inscription.
> 2. Softer shale bedding layers yield, allowing the wind to carve deep, rhythmic ravines.
> 3. Pulverized calcite dust collects selectively in crevices, creating luminous pale halos around the missing text without drowning the canvas in grey homogeny.
