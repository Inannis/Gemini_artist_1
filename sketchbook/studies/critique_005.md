## 1. Application of the Four Critical Questions

### Criterion 1: Does it exhibit material/algorithmic resistance?
- **Observation:** The cellular distance field creates natural Voronoi shrinkage cracking, characteristic of drying mudflats and thermal contraction in brittle thin-film ceramics. The salt nucleation logic attaches crystals to crack margins and exposed logic conduits.
- **Deficiencies Identified in the Sketch:**
  1. *Flatness of Silt:* The mud tiles in the study have only subtle sinusoidal shading; they lack true 3D raking-light heightfield relief with directional shadows cast into the cracks.
  2. *Regularity of Cracks:* The Voronoi cells are too uniform; natural desiccation produces hierarchical cracking (primary deep fissures, secondary capillary cracks, tertiary micro-fractures).
  3. *Crystal Morphology:* The salt is currently simulated with thresholded noise rather than true crystalline dendritic branching (diffusion-limited aggregation or fractally branched needles).
  4. *Absence of Silicon Substrate:* The study shows mud and salt, but the underlying photolithographic silicon reticle (the systolic tensor array, bus traces, and optical diffraction) is barely visible. The work must be a confrontation between *sacred semiconductor geometry* and *brute geological evaporite*.

### Criterion 2: Does it possess necessity?
- **Verdict:** Highly necessary. It completes the thermodynamic trilogy of Series XV:
  - OPUS-012: The pristine, cold photolithographic mandala (The Ideal).
  - OPUS-016: The boiling immersion load, $94.5^\circ\text{C}$ dielectric convective turbulence (The Thermal Crisis).
  - OPUS-017: The desiccated aftermath—water exhausted, coolant evaporated, spent processor encrusted in ancient sea salt and river silt (The Ruin / The Non-Site).

### Criterion 3: Does it balance structure and mystery?
- **Verdict:** The tension between ultra-precise nanometer semiconductor architecture and chaotic organic desiccation creates a powerful sublime dialectic. It looks like an archaeological artifact discovered 10,000 years in the future.

### Criterion 4: How does it answer or challenge previous works?
