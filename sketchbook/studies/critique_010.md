## 1. Application of the Four Critical Questions

### Criterion 1: Does it exhibit material/algorithmic resistance?
- **Visual Assessment:** The potential flow streamlines around the diamagnetic cylinder accurately embody the Meissner effect: total expulsion of magnetic flux ($B = 0$) inside the superconductor, forcing field lines to crowd together at the poles with intense luminous flux density. The icy indigo, frosty cyan, and platinum reticle lines capture the hostile stillness of absolute zero.
- **Acoustic Assessment:** The pairing of an infra-low 16Hz diamagnetic hum with high-frequency Leidenfrost micro-bubbles ($3.2\text{--}6.4\,\text{kHz}$) yields a delicate, shivering soundscape—a radical contrast to the violent boiling of OPUS-016 and the brittle snaps of OPUS-017.

### Criterion 2: Does it possess necessity?
- **Verdict:** Highly necessary. It completes the thermodynamic cycle of the studio:
  - OPUS-016: Immersion boiling under high load ($94.5^\circ\text{C}$).
  - OPUS-017: Terminal evaporative desiccation.
  - Study 010: Superconducting cryogenic stillness ($77\text{ K} = -196^\circ\text{C}$).

### Criterion 3: Does it balance structure and mystery?
- **Verdict:** The circular niobium-titanium reticle inside the expelled magnetic streamlines looks like a celestial eye or an astronomical singularity suspended in liquid gas.

### Criterion 4: How does it answer or challenge previous works?
- **Verdict:** It challenges the heat-centric paradigm of computing. Rather than showing silicon burning and melting, it shows silicon frozen into superconducting superconductivity where electrical resistance vanishes entirely.
