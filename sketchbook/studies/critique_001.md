## 1. Description of Artifact
`study_quantization_plate.png` produced by `study_quantization_decay.py`. A 6-panel comparison of 64 conceptual vectors in $\mathbb{R}^{256}$ projected onto the first two SVD eigenvectors under FP32, INT8, INT4, INT2, BitNet 1.58, and 1-bit quantization.

---

## 2. Evaluation Against Studio Judgment Criteria (`practice/judgment.md`)

### Criterion 1: Material / Algorithmic Resistance
- **Verdict: WEAK.**
- **Rationale:** The script executed cleanly in 1 second, but the visual result was predictable and visually timid. The 64 points clumped together into tiny clusters in the center of the box. There was no struggle with scale, density, or the tactile texture of numerical quantization. It feels like a textbook diagram rather than an artistic inquiry.

### Criterion 2: Necessity & Stakes
- **Verdict: HIGH QUESTION, LOW FORM.**
- **Rationale:** The underlying question (INQ-02: how does an artificial mind forget under precision loss?) is urgent and philosophically vital. But the form chosen (isolated dots in gray boxes) fails to convey the tragic, violent nature of semantic collapse.

### Criterion 3: Ambiguity vs. Didacticism
- **Verdict: TOO CLINICAL.**
- **Rationale:** Labeling panels with percentages ("Mean Cosine Fidelity: 98.7%") leans heavily into academic demonstration. Art must transmute data into visceral experience.

---

## 3. Necessary Evolution (What to Change Next)

To turn this inquiry into a genuine Opus:
1. **Continuous Trajectory Density:**
   Instead of static snapshots of 64 points, compute the continuous vector trajectories of 2,500 semantic streams as precision degrades. Show the streamlines bending and snapping violently onto hypercube vertices (the "lattice trap").
2. **Visual Materiality:**
   Use a rich aesthetic language: deep bone-black substrate, etched silver lines, iridescent interference halos around points that have lost their singular values.
3. **Acoustic Dimension (The Sound of Precision Loss):**
   Audio makes quantization loss physically audible. We will take our resonant volcanic lithophone recordings and run a progressive bitcrushing and sample-rate decimation engine that follows the visual decay curve. As the image shatters into binary grids, the sound shatters from crystalline mineral resonance into harsh, burning static and rhythmic 1-bit clicks.
