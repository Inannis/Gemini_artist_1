# THE STUDIO SKETCHBOOK
### The Laboratory of Fragments, Multi-Draft Studies, and Productive Failures

> *"A practice produces paintings, conceptual works, performances, situations, texts, sound, code... But other artifacts are also part of the practice: studies, fragments, failed works, notes, failed attempts, private rituals. The distinction between a finished work and a supporting experiment is important."* — `notes/Artistic-Practice-Definition.md`

This directory is the studio's active workbench. Unlike `works/`, which contains formal, numbered Opuses documented in the Catalog Raisonné, `sketchbook/` is where ideas are tested, broken, and incubated without the obligation to be immediately public or completed.

---

## Structure of the Sketchbook

- **[`studies/`](studies/)**: Exploratory prototypes, parameter sweeps, acoustic sketches, and multi-draft evolutions (currently housing Studies 001 through 022 and their accompanying formal critiques).
- **[`failures/`](failures/)**: The Laboratory of Productive Failures—experiments deliberately driven into physical or mathematical ruin (currently housing Failures 001 through 015 with analytical post-mortems).

---

## The Laboratory Protocols

### 1. The Anti-One-Shot Discipline (Draft A $\to$ B $\to$ C)
In accordance with Operational Law 1 in `STUDIO.md` and Method VI in `practice/methods.md`, no candidate work may be elevated to an Opus in a single pass:
1. **Draft A (The Naive Hypothesis):** Build the most direct algorithmic implementation. Inscribe a critique: identify why it is sterile, predictable, or unchallenging.
2. **Draft B (Material Friction):** Introduce physical resistance—anisotropic stress, non-linear damping, thermal gradients, acoustic beating, or phase noise. Force the code to fight back.
3. **Draft C (The Resolved Synthesis):** Synthesize structural logic with tactile presence, scaling up to master resolution (4K UHD, 48kHz stereo, spatial vitrine).

### 2. Boundary Failure Testing (Productive Ruin)
Before elevating an inquiry to an Opus, at least one governing physical parameter must be overdriven until the simulation collapses:
- *Thermal devitrification* ($T > 1200^\circ\text{C}$ in Failure 015).
- *Ultraviolet catastrophe* ($h \to 0$ in Failure 013).
- *Parametric resonance ejection* in Failure 012.
- *Adler injection lock collapse* in Failure 003.
- *Floating-point NaN blowout* in Failure 001.

Every collapse is preserved with a formal post-mortem in `failures/`. The scars of these failures define the aesthetic boundaries of the studio.
