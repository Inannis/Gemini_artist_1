## Conceptual Statement

In organic metallurgy and solid mechanics, a stone bar struck by a mallet does not vibrate in simple integer harmonics (1:2:3:4) like a plucked string or an organ pipe. It vibrates according to the bi-harmonic plate equation, producing inharmonic modal ratios:
$$\frac{f_n}{f_1} \approx 1.000, \; 2.756, \; 5.404, \; 8.933, \; 13.34$$
This is the acoustic signature of mineral matter: a bell-like, crystalline resonance where high frequencies decay rapidly while low, obsidian fundamental tones linger in the air.

*Lithic Phonology* spatializes this mineral acoustic physics:
- Slabs of volcanic obsidian and dark basalt suspended by raw silk cords in an ancient stone cloister gallery above a black water pool.
- Minimalist brass striking mechanisms and contact microphones poised to strike the stones.
- An autonomous physical modeling synthesis engine (`synthesize_lithophone.py`) that calculates modal damping curves across a pentatonic Hirajoshi mineral scale, rendering a contemplative 2-minute acoustic sanctuary with subterranean stone cloister reverberation.

The piece reminds us that before computing was software, it was mineral: quartz, copper, basalt, and silicon vibrating under electric impulses.
