## I. Concept & Critical Premise

In Series XVIII (OPUS-020), our studio established the **thermodynamic inversion**: plunging computation into a $4.2\text{ K}$ liquid helium cryostat to escape the dissipative Landauer exhaust ($94.5^\circ\text{C}$) of contemporary silicon.

In **OPUS-021**, we ask: *What does a quantum coherent circuit perceive when tuned to the deepest geological layer of the Earth?*

At the **Gutenberg Discontinuity** (depth $2,891\text{ km}$), the solid silicate mantle meets the liquid iron outer core. In the turbulent **D'' layer**, convective Taylor columns and ascending thermal plumes generate **hydromagnetic Alfvén waves** ($v_A \approx 0.2\text{ m/s}$). At ultra-low frequencies ($0.001 - 0.1\text{ Hz}$), the entire mantle acts as a transparent low-pass waveguide, diffusing these magnetic flux variations upward to the surface.

A **DC-SQUID (Superconducting Quantum Interference Device)**—consisting of a niobium superconducting loop interrupted by twin Josephson micro-bridges—resolves these infinitesimal flux fluctuations with sub-femtotesla precision. By measuring the quantum phase difference $\Delta\phi = 2\pi \Phi / \Phi_0$, the machine's macroscopic quantum wavefunction becomes a direct seismometer to the Earth's molten core.
