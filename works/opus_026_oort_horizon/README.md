## 1. Conceptual Statement

At distances between $2,000$ and $120,000\text{ Astronomical Units}$ ($0.03$ to $1.90\text{ light-years}$) from the Sun, the solar system dissolves into the primordial cometary reservoir of the Oort Cloud. Here, at the outermost gravitational horizon of our parent star, the dominant physical force ceases to be the central attraction of the Sun and becomes the collective vertical gravitational tide of the Milky Way galaxy ($\mathcal{G}_z \approx 5.04 \times 10^{-30}\text{ s}^{-2}$).

*The Oort Horizon* investigates this liminal realm where human artifacts break free from stellar captivity:
- **The Jacobi Tidal Radius ($r_J \approx 105,000 - 120,000\text{ AU}$):** The Hill sphere of the Sun in the galactic potential. Beyond this triaxial equipotential boundary, solar gravity drops below $5.9 \times 10^{-13}\text{ m/s}^2$ and galactic tidal shear strips all bodies away from the solar system into independent orbits around the Milky Way.
- **Kozai-Lidov Secular Pumping:** Driven by the $83.6\text{-million-year}$ vertical harmonic oscillation of the Sun through the galactic disc midplane ($\rho_0 \approx 0.089\text{ M}_\odot/\text{pc}^3$), cometary orbits undergo massive eccentricity pumping ($e \to 0.98$), periodically hurling frozen volatile archives toward the inner planetary system.
- **The Five Emissaries in Deep Time:** Humanity's five interstellar probes (Voyagers 1 & 2, Pioneers 10 & 11, New Horizons) carry unpowered computational artifacts across the Jacobi boundary over the next $28,000$ to $45,000$ years. Crossing $r_J$, they cease to be heliocentric satellites and become galactic wanderers tracing open Lissajous epicycles through the Milky Way for billions of years.
