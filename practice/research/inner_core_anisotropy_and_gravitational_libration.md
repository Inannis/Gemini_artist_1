## II. Mineral Physics: Hexagonal Close-Packed $\varepsilon$-Iron Anisotropy

At pressures exceeding $13\text{ GPa}$, pure iron undergoes a polymorphic phase transition from body-centered cubic ($\alpha$-iron) to hexagonal close-packed ($\varepsilon$-iron). In the extreme conditions of Earth's center ($P \approx 330 - 364\text{ GPa}$, $T \approx 5,500 - 6,000\text{ K}$), the solid inner core is composed primarily of crystalline $\varepsilon\text{-Fe}$ alloyed with $\approx 5\text{ wt}\%\text{ Ni}$ and light elements (Si, S, C, O, H).

### 1. Elastic Anisotropy of the $\varepsilon$-Iron Single Crystal
The elastic stiffness tensor $C_{ijkl}$ of single-crystal hcp iron possesses hexagonal symmetry, characterized by five independent elastic constants ($C_{11}, C_{33}, C_{12}, C_{13}, C_{44}$).
First-principles molecular dynamics and diamond anvil cell measurements demonstrate that compressional sound waves travel significantly faster along the crystallographic $c$-axis $[0001]$ than in the basal $a$-plane $[11\bar{2}0]$:
$$v_p(c\text{-axis}) = \sqrt{\frac{C_{33}}{\rho}} \approx 11.8\text{ km/s}$$
$$v_p(a\text{-axis}) = \sqrt{\frac{C_{11}}{\rho}} \approx 10.9\text{ km/s}$$

### 2. Macroscopic Preferred Orientation (CPO)
During inner core solidification, Maxwell magnetic stresses, geostrophic flow shear, and thermal convection align the microscopic iron crystallites into a global preferred orientation (lattice preferred orientation or LPO).
Macroscopically, the inner core behaves as a transversely isotropic medium whose fast symmetry axis is tilted only $\approx 0.5^\circ - 1.0^\circ$ from Earth's rotation axis. Compressional seismic waves ($PKIKP$) traversing the inner core along north-south polar paths travel at $11.372\text{ km/s}$, whereas equatorial paths travel at $11.030\text{ km/s}$—a persistent **$+3.1\%$ polar anisotropy**.

## III. Differential Super-Rotation and Seismic Doublet Interferometry

Because the solid inner core is mechanically decoupled from the mantle by the surrounding low-viscosity liquid outer core ($\eta \sim 10^{-3}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$), it is free to rotate independently about the planetary spin axis.

### 1. The Song & Richards Discovery (1996)
In 1996, Xiaodong Song and Paul G. Richards discovered that seismic waves passing through the inner core from repeating earthquakes in the South Sandwich Islands to seismic station COL (College, Alaska) arrived slightly faster year after year. They inferred that the inner core was rotating eastward faster than the mantle—a "super-rotation" of $\approx 1.1^\circ$ per year.

### 2. Seismic Doublets: Repeating Earthquakes as Precision Clocks
A **seismic doublet** consists of two distinct earthquakes occurring at the exact same geographic hypocenter and fault rupture patch, separated by years or decades. Because the source and receiver positions are identical, any difference in the recorded seismogram must be caused by temporal changes in the medium traversed by the wave.

For the core phase $PKIKP$ (which refracts through the inner core), the travel-time difference $\Delta t$ between doublet events separated by $\Delta \tau$ reflects the rotation of the inner core's anisotropic velocity gradient:
$$\Delta t = - \frac{L}{v_p^2} \left( \frac{\partial v_p}{\partial \phi} \right) \Delta \phi$$
where $L \approx 2,400\text{ km}$ is the path length through the inner core, and $\Delta \phi = \Omega_{\text{rel}} \Delta \tau$ is the angular rotation accumulated during the interval.

### 3. The 65-Year Multidecadal Oscillation (Yang & Song 2023)
Recent global doublet analyses (Yang & Song 2023, Vidale 2024) revealed that inner core super-rotation is not a monotonic, unidirectional drift. Around 2009–2010, the travel-time residuals reached an inflection point and reversed slope.

The inner core is executing a **multidecadal oscillation** with a period of $T \approx 65.0\text{ years}$ and an angular amplitude of $\Delta \phi_{\text{max}} \approx \pm 1.25^\circ$:
$$\phi(t) = \phi_0 + A \sin \left( \frac{2\pi}{T_{\text{osc}}} (t - t_0) \right)$$
In our current epoch (2026.69), the inner core is accelerating eastward from its 2020 western turning point at $+0.0839^\circ/\text{year}$, producing a $+5.12\text{ ms}$ doublet arrival delay relative to baseline.

## IV. Gravitational Coupling: The Planetary Restoring Spring

What bounds the inner core to this 65-year oscillation instead of allowing it to spin freely?

### 1. Gravitational Torque $\Gamma_g$
The lower mantle has lateral density variations (associated with subducted lithospheric slabs and Large Low Shear Velocity Provinces / LLSVPs), generating a non-axisymmetric gravitational potential $V_{\text{mantle}}(r, \theta, \phi)$.
Similarly, the inner core has non-axisymmetric topography and density variations $\rho_1(r, \theta, \phi)$ frozen into its crystalline lattice.

When the inner core rotates by an angle $\phi$ relative to the mantle, their respective gravitational mass anomalies misalign, generating an angular restoring torque (Buffett 1996):
$$\Gamma_g(\phi) = - K_g \sin(2\phi) \approx - 2 K_g \phi$$
where $K_g \approx 3.0 \times 10^{20}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/rad}$.

### 2. The Inner-Core Libration Equation
The equation of motion for the inner core's relative rotation is:
$$I_{\text{IC}} \frac{d^2\phi}{dt^2} + D \frac{d\phi}{dt} + 2 K_g \phi = \Gamma_{\text{EM}}(t)$$
where:
- $I_{\text{IC}} = \frac{2}{5} M R^2 \approx 5.83 \times 10^{34}\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ is the inner core's moment of inertia.
- $D$ is viscous and electromagnetic damping at the inner core boundary.
- $\Gamma_{\text{EM}}(t)$ is the electromagnetic torque exerted by outer core magnetohydrodynamic convection.

The natural period of this gravitational oscillator is:
$$T_0 = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{I_{\text{IC}}}{2 K_g}} \approx 62 - 68\text{ years}$$
This matches the observed 65-year multidecadal seismic doublet oscillation with extraordinary precision!
