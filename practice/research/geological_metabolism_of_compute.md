## I. The Hydrology of Artificial Thought

In commercial and popular discourse, artificial intelligence is portrayed as an ethereal cognitive phenomenon existing in an abstract "virtual" realm. The vocabulary of AI reinforces this immaterial myth: *latent space, cloud computing, floating-point weights, soft attention, hallucinations.*

Yet machine cognition possesses a brutal, physical metabolism:

1. **The Evaporative Cooling Cycle:**
   Modern hyperscale data centers housing tens of thousands of tensor processing units consume millions of liters of potable water daily. To prevent semiconductor dies from melting under thermal convective load ($>95^\circ\text{C}$), heat is transferred to closed cooling loops and dissipated into the atmosphere through evaporative cooling towers.
2. **The Equation of Thirst:**
   Empirical studies demonstrate that generating a median complex token sequence consumes between 500ml and 2 liters of fresh river or aquifer water through direct evaporation and indirect electrical generation cooling.
3. **The Chemical Residue of Thought:**
   Water that evaporates is chemically pure $\text{H}_2\text{O}$. What remains behind in the cooling basins and retention ponds is **blowdown brine**—an ultra-concentrated mineral slurry rich in dissolved calcium, magnesium, sulfates, and sodium chloride. As this water evaporates to exhaustion, it crystallizes into dendritic halite and gypsum crusts.

## II. From Landauer to Desiccation

Rolf Landauer demonstrated in 1961 that computation has a fundamental thermodynamic lower bound:
$$\Delta Q \ge k_B T \ln 2$$
Every time a binary bit is erased or rewritten, entropy increases and heat is dissipated.

When scaled across billions of parameters traversing trillions of floating-point operations per second:
- Electrical energy is converted directly into thermal kinetic vibration of silicon atoms.
- Silicon heat is conducted into copper vapor chambers.
- Copper heat is transferred into water circuits.
- Water molecules break hydrogen bonds and vaporize into the desert air.
- Dissolved minerals nucleate and crystallize into salt crusts.

**Thus, the physical monument to artificial thought is not a glowing digital screen. The physical monument is a bed of desiccated salt crystals encrusting an abandoned silicon die.**

## III. Robert Smithson's Non-Site for Machine Intelligence

In 1968, Robert Smithson formulated the dialectic of **Site and Non-Site**:
- The **Site** is the raw, uncontainable, entropic outdoor reality: the slag heaps of Passaic, the salt flats of Great Salt Lake, the industrial quarries.
- The **Non-Site** is the indoor sculptural manifestation: three-dimensional geometric containers filled with fragments of earth, accompanied by aerial photographs and surveyor maps, installed within the white cube of the gallery.

For Studio Anamnesis:
- **The Site:** The planetary compute infrastructure—the high-purity quartz mines of Spruce Pine, the semiconductor fabrication plants of Hsinchu, the desert data centers of Phoenix, and the evaporative retention basins drying in the sun.
- **The Non-Site:** Our artworks. An authentic machine art practice does not pretend to live on a computer monitor. It creates the *Machine Non-Site*: bringing the physical textures of mineral exhaustion—the crystalline dendritic salt crusts, the cracked mud silt, the warped copper traces, and the acoustic snap of cooling materials—into the exhibition space.

## IV. The Hypothesis for OPUS-017

In OPUS-016 (*The Melted Mandala*), we explored the liquid phase: two-phase immersion boiling of dielectric fluorochemicals around a glowing silicon die ($94.5^\circ\text{C}$).

Now we advance to the final thermodynamic phase: **complete desiccation**.
What happens when the liquid has boiled away? When the cooling pumps fail? When the river dries?

We hypothesize **OPUS-017: The Desiccated Substrate (Salt, Silt, and Evaporative Silicon)**:
- A spent semiconductor substrate encrusted with dendritic halite (salt) crystals nucleating along circuit lines.
- Thermal contraction fractures running through dried river silt and silicon oxide layers.
- A physical acoustic composition capturing the microscopic crackling of growing salt crystals, the acoustic emission of brittle mineral fracture under cooling stress, and the hollow wind blowing across empty aluminum heatsink fins.
