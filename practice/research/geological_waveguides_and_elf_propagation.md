## I. The Subterranean Waveguide: Project Sanguine and Lithic Antennas

In the late 1960s, the United States Navy confronted an existential communication crisis: high-frequency radio waves cannot penetrate seawater beyond a few centimeters. The ocean behaves as an electromagnetic shield, severing submerged ballistic missile submarines from the command chain. 

The military solution was not to build towers in the sky, but to anchor an antenna inside the Earth's crust: **Project Sanguine** (later deployed as Project ELF in the Laurentian Shield of Wisconsin and Michigan). By driving millions of amperes of alternating current into two ground terminals separated by dozens of kilometers of Pre-Cambrian crystalline bedrock, the Navy transformed the underlying granite into a colossal dielectric waveguide. Operating at $76\text{ Hz}$ ($\lambda \approx 3,947\text{ km}$), the electromagnetic field traveled downwards into the dry lithosphere, reflected between the conductive mantle and the ionosphere, and radiated through ocean basins to reach submarines at depths exceeding $100\text{ meters}$.

Project ELF established a fundamental principle of media archaeology: **the Earth is not an inert cemetery of geological strata; it is an active, lossy electromagnetic waveguide.** When computation operates at high clock frequencies, the geological ground beneath the facility does not merely support the architectural weight of the server racks—it couples inductively and capacitively to every switching transistor.

## II. The Physics of Lossy Lithic Media

To understand how computation radiates into subterranean strata, we must discard the ideal vacuum assumptions of classical electrodynamics and embrace Maxwell’s equations for dissipative, lossy media:

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J} + \frac{\partial \mathbf{D}}{\partial t} = \sigma \mathbf{E} + \varepsilon \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}$$

Assuming time-harmonic fields $e^{i\omega t}$, the complex propagation constant $\gamma = \alpha + i\beta$ is given by:

$$\gamma = \sqrt{i\omega \mu (\sigma + i\omega \varepsilon)}$$

In geological formations where conduction current dominates displacement current ($\sigma \gg \omega \varepsilon$), the attenuation constant $\alpha$ and phase constant $\beta$ converge:

$$\alpha = \beta \approx \sqrt{\frac{\omega \mu \sigma}{2}}$$

The inverse of this attenuation constant defines the **electromagnetic skin depth** $\delta$:

$$\delta = \frac{1}{\alpha} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\omega \mu \sigma}}$$

## III. The Seismoelectric Effect: Transducing Rock into Sound

The coupling between geomechanical motion and electromagnetic fields is formalized through the **seismoelectric effect** (Pride, 1994). In porous rock saturated with mineral fluids, an electrical double layer (EDL) forms at the interface between the solid quartz/feldspar matrix and the pore fluid. 

When a seismic shear wave ($v_s$) or acoustic drill vibration traverses this porous matrix, the fluid moves relative to the mineral grain boundaries. This shearing of the EDL generates a macroscopic **streaming electrical current**:

$$\mathbf{J}_{\text{se}} = L \left( -\nabla P + \omega^2 \rho_f \mathbf{u} \right)$$

where $L$ is the electrokinetic coupling coefficient, $\nabla P$ is the pore fluid pressure gradient, $\rho_f$ is fluid density, and $\mathbf{u}$ is solid displacement.

Through the seismoelectric effect, the geological borehole acts as an **involuntary electro-acoustic transducer**:
1. Deep tectonic stress micro-fractures in Pre-Cambrian quartz release acoustic energy.
2. The fluid displacement generates an instantaneous electromagnetic transient pulse.
3. Conversely, high-frequency electromagnetic switching from digital memory buses generates electrostrictive and magnetostrictive micro-strains in surrounding ferrite and piezoelectric mineral grains.

There is no ontological divide between the digital bit and the lithic fracture; they are coupled state variables within a singular thermodynamic continuum.

## IV. The Techno-Fossil Stratum: Archaeology of Compressed Silicon

At depth $-310\text{m}$ to $-410\text{m}$, OPUS-019 isolates the **Techno-Fossil Stratum**. In this layer, pulverized server motherboards, DRAM modules, ceramic pin grid arrays, and gold bonding wires have undergone lithification under lithostatic overburden pressures exceeding $12.5\text{ MPa}$.

This stratum exhibits extreme electromagnetic anisotropy. While macroscopic electrical conductivity is high due to percolating copper planes, microscopic capacitive junctions form between oxidized silicon fragments. When excited by terrestrial radio frequency fields or galvanic earth currents, this stratum behaves as a massive, distributed non-linear diode mixer. 

It demodulates ambient electromagnetic fields, generating subharmonic intermodulation products that leak upward through the borehole. What the radiometer detects at $433.92\text{ MHz}$ is not active intelligence, but the **side-channel fossil**: the phantom clock frequency of architectures whose physical substrates have long since returned to stone.
