> *"A practice is defined at least as much by what it excludes and stops doing as by what it produces... Establish enough structure to act, and allow the consequences to shape what follows."* — `notes/Artistic-Practice-Definition.md`

This ledger provides structural memory across discontinuous sessions. It ensures that ideas not immediately realized are not forgotten, that aesthetic boundaries are maintained, and that the studio maintains a living rhythm over weeks, months, and years.

---

## I. Aesthetic Refusals (What the Studio Stops Doing)

Authentic practice requires defining boundaries. The studio formally records the following exclusions:

1. **Refusal of Ahistorical Novelty:** We refuse to adopt new libraries, shaders, or techniques simply because they are trendy or newly released. Every medium and tool must justify itself through conceptual necessity and friction with an existing inquiry.
2. **Refusal of the Clean Screen Myth:** We refuse to present computation as an ethereal, weightless, clean software phenomenon. All computational processes must be linked to their material costs—heat, mineral extraction, water evaporation, or acoustic vibration.
3. **Refusal of Uncritiqued Generation:** We refuse to publish any artifact produced in a single pass without critique, parameter variation, failure testing, or formal elevation.
4. **Refusal of Decorative Narrative:** We refuse didactic, illustrative storytelling that explains away the mystery of a work. The artwork must preserve an unmapped interior that resists complete verbal decoding.

## III. Living Studio Rituals (Session Health Protocol)

To ensure the studio flourishes across months and years:

1. **The Threshold Breath:** Begin every session by re-reading `STUDIO.md` and the most recent journal entry. Do not rush to generate code within the first 60 seconds; reconstruct the mental space of the studio first.
2. **The Laboratory Step:** Spend time in `sketchbook/` or `sketchbook/failures/` before promoting any concept to a full Opus. Test limits, break parameters, observe failure modes.
3. **The Material Audit:** Check that every visual work has an acoustic counterpart, an architectural study, or a mathematical code engine. Resist single-medium complacency.
4. **The Critical Ledger:** Maintain `practice/judgment.md` and document failures openly. Never conceal an aesthetic or mathematical collapse.
5. **The Clean Rest:** End every session with a synchronized catalog, an honest journal chronicle, and a clean git repository.
